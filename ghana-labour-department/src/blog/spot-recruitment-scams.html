An offer that sounds too good to be true

When you need work badly, an exciting offer can feel like an answer to prayer. Sadly, some people use that hope to take money. You can protect yourself.

The Ghana Labour Department never charges for jobs. Our services are free. If anyone asks you to pay for a job, treat it as a warning sign.

This guide shows you the warning signs. It also explains how to check an agency and what to do if you are targeted.

Warning signs of a recruitment scam

Watch out for these signs:

  • You are asked to pay for a job. Someone says you must send money before you can start work.
  • A "visa lottery". They promise you a visa or a place abroad if you pay to enter.
  • "Processing fees". Scammers use names like this to make a payment sound official.
  • No written contract. A genuine employer gives you a contract to read before you agree.
  • Pressure to travel fast. They say the offer ends today, or you must leave this week.
  • They take your passport. They ask to keep your passport "for safety" or "for processing".
  • The agency is not registered. It cannot show a licence from the Department.

If you see even one of these signs, stop. Check before you go any further.

Only use licensed Private Employment Agencies

A Private Employment Agency (PEA) is a business that helps people find jobs. Some PEAs find jobs in Ghana. Others find jobs abroad.

Under the Labour Act, 2003 (Act 651), section 7, a PEA must be licensed by the Department. A licence shows that the Department has approved the agency to operate.

An unlicensed agency puts your money and your safety at risk. You may have no contract and no one to turn to if things go wrong. Learn more on our Private Employment Agencies page.

How to check an agency

Checking takes only a few minutes. It could save you a lot of money and pain.

  1. Ask the agency to show you its licence from the Department.
  2. Write down the agency's name and its licence details.
  3. Confirm the licence with your nearest Labour Department office. Find one in our PEC directory.
  4. Or call us toll-free on 0800 600 300 and ask us to check.

A genuine agency will not mind you checking. If they refuse, or get angry, that is a warning sign.

What to do if you are targeted

You may feel ashamed or angry if you think you have been targeted. Please do not blame yourself. Scammers are clever, and it can happen to anyone.

Acting quickly can protect you and others. Follow these steps:

  1. Stop paying. Do not send any more money, even if they promise a refund.
  2. Keep evidence. Save receipts, messages, phone numbers, adverts and names.
  3. Report it to us toll-free on 0800 600 300 or 0800 600 400.
  4. If money was taken, report it to the Police as well.

Your report helps us warn others. You can also read more about your rights at work.

Remember: The Department never charges for jobs. If someone asks for money in our name, it is a scam. Call us toll-free on 0800 600 300.

Find genuine jobs for free

You do not need to pay anyone to find work. Genuine local vacancies are listed free on the Ghana Labour Market Information System (GLMIS).

Visit glmis.gov.gh to create a profile and search for jobs. Our guide on how to register on GLMIS shows you how, step by step.

You can also visit your nearest Public Employment Centre (PEC) for free help. Read about the five ways your PEC can help.
