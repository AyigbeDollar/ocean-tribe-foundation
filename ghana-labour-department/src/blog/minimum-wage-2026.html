The new rate at a glance

Money worries are real for many families. When prices go up, every cedi counts. So it is important to know what the law says about your pay.

From 1 January 2026, the National Daily Minimum Wage is GH¢21.77 a day. This is the lowest amount an employer can pay for a day's work.

The new rate is 9% higher than the 2025 rate of GH¢19.97 a day.

The Ghana Labour Department wants every worker to know this rate. Knowing it helps you check that you are paid fairly.

Who sets the minimum wage?

The National Tripartite Committee sets the rate. "Tripartite" means three parties. The committee brings together:

  • government
  • employers
  • organised labour (trade unions that speak for workers)

The committee does this under the Labour Act, 2003 (Act 651), section 113. The three groups meet and agree a rate together. This means workers and employers both have a voice.

Who does it cover?

The minimum wage applies to all workers in Ghana. It does not matter where you work. It covers work in an office, a shop, a factory, a farm or a home.

Your employer must not pay you below GH¢21.77 for a day's work. The minimum wage is a floor, not a target. Many employers pay more, and you can always agree higher pay.

What GH¢21.77 a day can mean in a month

Your monthly pay depends on how many days you work. It also depends on what your contract says. Here is a simple example to help you check.

Example only: you work 26 days in a month at the minimum wage.

26 days x GH¢21.77 = GH¢566.02

This is only an illustration. Your actual pay depends on the days you work and the terms of your contract.

To check your own pay, follow these steps:

  1. Count the days you worked in the pay period.
  2. Multiply that number by GH¢21.77.
  3. Compare the answer with your pay before any deductions.

Is your daily pay lower than GH¢21.77? Then you may be underpaid.

What to do if you are paid less

Finding out you are underpaid can be upsetting. You may worry about losing your job if you speak up. We understand. You have the right to fair pay, and we can help.

  1. Raise the problem with your employer in writing. A short letter or message is enough. Keep a copy.
  2. Keep your payslips, your contract and any record of the days you worked.
  3. Visit your nearest Labour Department office or Public Employment Centre (PEC). Our Labour Officers offer free mediation.

Mediation means we sit down with you and your employer. Together, we look for a fair solution. It costs you nothing.

Learn how to make a complaint on our complaints page. Find an office near you in our PEC directory.

Remember: Keep every payslip. Written records make it easier for us to help you.

A note for employers

If you employ staff, check your payroll now. From 1 January 2026, every worker must receive at least GH¢21.77 a day.

Paying the minimum wage is the law. It also builds trust with your workers. If you have questions about your duties, contact your nearest Labour Department office. Our Labour Officers can explain what the Labour Act, 2003 (Act 651) requires.

Looking for staff? You can post vacancies free on the Ghana Labour Market Information System (GLMIS) at glmis.gov.gh.
