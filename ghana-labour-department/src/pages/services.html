Our services

Practical help for job seekers, workers and employers. Most of our services are free of charge.

Public employment services

Register on GLMIS

Our Public Employment Centres (PECs) offer free job matching, career guidance and registration on the Ghana Labour Market Information System (GLMIS). There is a regional PEC in each of Ghana's 16 regions, with district offices in many areas.

  • Job seekers: register on GLMIS online, or visit a PEC for help creating your profile and CV.
  • Employers: post vacancies on GLMIS and ask a PEC to shortlist candidates at no cost.
  • Career counselling: advice on training, skills and how to apply.

Labour complaints and mediation

If you have a problem at work, such as unpaid salary, dismissal without notice, denied leave or unsafe conditions, we can help you and your employer reach a fair settlement.

  1. Try to resolve the issue with your supervisor or HR, and keep a record.
  2. Visit the nearest Labour Department office or Public Employment Centre. Bring your appointment letter or contract, payslips, any dismissal letter and your Ghana Card.
  3. A Labour Officer records your complaint, invites your employer and mediates. Mediation is free.
  4. If no agreement is reached, the Labour Officer advises you on your further options under the law.

Complaints about unfair termination should be made promptly. Ask a Labour Officer about time limits that apply to your case.

Private employment agency licensing

Under section 7 of the Labour Act, only a company licensed by the Minister may operate a private employment agency, including agencies that recruit Ghanaians for jobs abroad.

  1. Incorporate your company with the Office of the Registrar of Companies.
  2. Submit an application to the Minister through the Labour Department, with the required documents.
  3. Officers inspect your premises, facilities and staff.
  4. On approval you receive a licence, which must be renewed.
  5. Licensed agencies submit returns to the Chief Labour Officer within 14 days after every quarter. Failure to do so can lead to the licence being revoked.

Job seekers: before paying any recruiter, ask to see their Labour Department licence and confirm it with your nearest Labour Department office. Offers of guaranteed jobs or visas abroad for a fee are a common scam.

Workmen's compensation

The Workmen's Compensation Act, 1987 (PNDCL 187) makes employers liable to compensate workers injured, made ill or killed through their work. The Labour Department administers claims.

  1. The employer reports the accident or occupational disease to the Labour Department.
  2. The worker receives medical treatment and a medical assessment of any incapacity.
  3. The Department assesses the claim and the compensation due.
  4. The employer, or the employer's insurer, pays the compensation.

Injured workers or their families can visit any Labour Department office for help if an employer has not reported an accident.

Labour inspection
